The Engineer's Promise

Core Principles of The Engineer's Promise

The 4 core principles of the Engineer's promise are;

  1. Acting with Integrity Principle
    Acting with Integrity honesty and fairness.
  2. Acting in the Public Good Principle
    Using engineering skills for the public good with public safety always the first priority.
  3. Protect the Environment Principle
    Using Earth's resources wisely, acting with professionalism and at all times using engineering skills to protect the environment and human well-being.
  4. Do No Harm Principle
    Acting professionally and at all times mindful to avoid causing unintentional negative impacts

The Engineer's Promise - background

Not many people aware that;

The 4 core principles of the Engineer's promise are now over 100 years old.

They were originally drafted in 1922 by Rudyard Kipling for Canadian graduate engineers.

See link;

https://ironring.ca/background-en/

Canadian Engineers, to this day, on graduating receive a steel ring ( now stainless), originally forged from the steel of a Quebec bridge which tragically collapsed in in 1907 killing 75 workers and collapsed again in 1916.

The cause of the failures was due to design errors, rushed construction and financially driven short-cuts.

The steel ring is given to engineers, to remind them of the lessons learned from the tragedy, and their professional obligation to act with integrity, including to stand up for what is right.

This ring symbol is incorporated in in our Engineers Ireland logo for this reason also.
